## 2. Creative vs editorial, and releases

| | Creative | Editorial |
|---|---|---|
| Use | Advertising, marketing, products, anything commercial | News, magazines, books, blogs: telling a story |
| Releases | **Required** for every recognisable person (model release) and for private property and artworks (property release) | Not needed |
| Logos and brands | Must not be prominent | Fine |
| Captions | Descriptive; no dateline needed | Factual dateline: where, when, what |
| Price and reach | Broadest use, usually higher value per sale | Narrower use, but no release work |
| Customer requests and briefs | Almost always creative, so they need releases | Rarely |

Getty itself says creative content needs a release for any recognisable person or property, and that editorial images carry no releases and so can't be used commercially.

**What to do: decide at capture, not at rejection.**

1. **People recognisable, no release → editorial from the start.** Street scenes, markets, events, protests. Don't send them to creative and wait to be refused; that costs you a round trip and time-sensitive news value.
2. **No people, no logos, public view → creative.** Architecture details, plants, textures, empty streets. Add `No People`.
3. **People you can ask → creative with releases.** Family, friends, a stallholder who's happy to pose. Keep a few blank Getty model releases on the phone (Getty supplies the form; the Contributor app takes a photo of the signed form). This is the one change that opens up customer requests and briefs.
4. **Once a month, pick one brief you can actually shoot** with a released person. Small and regular beats a big push. I'll list suitable briefs in the weekly check-in if you paste them in.

Rule of thumb: *if in doubt, editorial*. A refused creative submission costs more than a slightly narrower licence.

## 4. Mobile photos into the flow

Simplest route with what you already use:

1. On the iPhone, add keepers to a **Lightroom mobile album called `Getty Inbox`**.
2. That album syncs to Lightroom Classic (under *From Lightroom*). You process it with everything else. There's no separate pipeline.

## 7. Showing more than the guided tours

Getty exclusivity isn't as strict as you've been treating it. Getty's exclusivity guidance allows contributors to show accepted work in their own portfolio and on social media for promotion. It recommends watermarks and checking each platform's terms, and the contributor agreement keeps rights for self-promotion. **Licensing or giving the images away is what's off-limits.** The blog's new 1200px cap, its right-click protection and a link from every photo to its Getty page fit that.

Recommendation, **less is more**: keep one site and add one strand.

- **On Foot → two kinds of walk.** *Guided* (as now) and *Self-guided*: the NotebookLM itineraries you follow, written up as photo-led posts with 8–12 pictures, short captions and the route. Same template, a filter on the homepage, every photo linked to Getty. These walks are your Getty shoots, so the post costs little extra work.
- **Occasional themed sets** (doors, shopfronts, street plates, markets), in the spirit of McCurry's blog: one subject, 10–15 pictures, a paragraph. Each one doubles as a Getty board.
- **Instagram:** one carousel a month from the newest post, linking to it. Nothing more.
- **Not now:** a separate photo diary, a newsletter, collaborations. Revisit when the blog has analytics.
